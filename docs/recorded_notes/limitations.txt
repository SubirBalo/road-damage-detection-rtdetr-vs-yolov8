PROJECT LIMITATIONS

1. EDGE COMPUTING PERFORMANCE
The NVIDIA Jetson Nano 4GB is a resource-constrained embedded
platform. RT-DETR-R18 TensorRT FP16 deployment achieved
approximately 2.2 FPS during live OAK-D operation, which is
substantially slower than the RTX 5070 inference-only benchmark.

2. END-TO-END RECORDING OVERHEAD
The recorded demonstration achieved 1.892 FPS. This measurement
includes camera acquisition, preprocessing, TensorRT inference,
post-processing, annotation, display, and MP4 encoding, so it
should not be interpreted as pure model inference speed.

3. MODEL ACCURACY
The test-set mAP50-95 was 0.350 and AP50 was 0.644.
Performance varies between damage classes. D40 achieved the
highest AP50 (0.7572), while the Other class achieved the
lowest AP50 (0.5478).

4. RECALL
At confidence >= 0.60 and IoU >= 0.50, precision was 77.04%
and recall was 51.65%. Therefore, the selected threshold favors
higher-confidence detections but some road damage remains
undetected.

5. PHYSICAL DEMONSTRATION CONDITIONS
The OAK-D live demonstration included tests where road-damage
images were displayed on another screen and captured by the
camera. Perspective distortion, display scaling, reflections,
and screen/camera artifacts can affect classification results.
This is evidence of physical camera-to-edge-device operation,
but it is not equivalent to a controlled real-road field test.

6. CAMERA CONNECTION
The OAK-D operated through a USB 2 connection during the
validated deployment, which can limit available camera
bandwidth compared with USB 3 operation.

7. FUTURE WORK
Future development should include real-road data collection,
vehicle-mounted testing, additional training/data balancing,
threshold optimization, faster edge hardware or further model
optimization, and systematic latency/FPS measurements under
controlled conditions.
